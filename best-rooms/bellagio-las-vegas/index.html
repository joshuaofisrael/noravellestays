Fountain View King is the Bellagio room that faces the fountains

By Noravelle Stays Editorial. Sources checked 20 November 2026. Refresh owner: Travel site bot.

Which room to ask for at Bellagio in Las Vegas comes from the hotel's own room pages, not from a floor number. The Fountain View King page is the standard room that says you wake up to the fountains. The Penthouse Suite page is the suite that lists Fountain View, Strip View, or Sunset View, and it says the suite sits in the Bellagio Tower. Noravelle does not have a Las Vegas hotel city guide, so this page does not invent one. The parent list is best rooms.

Quick answer

Hotel confirms: Fountain View King is 510 square feet, one king bed, maximum three guests, with views of the fountains. The rooms index lists Fountain View Two Queen at 510 square feet with two queen beds and the same fountain line. Resort Tower King is 626 square feet with panoramic views and does not name the fountains. The room pages we opened do not publish a floor chart.

Our recommendation: If the fountain show is the reason for the stay, book Fountain View King or Fountain View Two Queen. If you want the choice of fountain, Strip, or sunset in writing, book the Penthouse Suite and name the view. Do not ask for a floor the hotel has not published.

Ask the hotel: Which of the three penthouse views is actually open, and whether your non-suite category is the one whose page names the fountains.

Best floors and views

Hotel confirms: The FAQ names two towers for accessible rooms: 22 accessible Deluxe Rooms in the Spa Tower and 63 accessible Resort Rooms in the Bellagio Tower. The only floor number in that FAQ is the 19th floor of the Bellagio Tower, and only as the place where certain accommodations are the smoking exception. The Fountain View King page, the Resort Tower King page, and the rooms index do not say which floors face the fountains. The FAQ also says hotel brochures do not contain pictures of the guestrooms.

Our recommendation: Treat the view as a room category, not a floor. Fountain View is the category that names the fountains. Resort Tower King is the category that buys space and a panoramic line without promising the show. Strip View, as a named option, shows up on the Penthouse Suite page, not on the 510 square foot king page.

Ask the hotel: If a fountain window matters, ask them to confirm the category on the reservation, not a floor. If smoke is the worry, ask whether the room is one of the 19th floor Bellagio Tower accommodations the FAQ exempts from the non-smoking rule.

Rooms and towers to request

Fountain View King, or Fountain View Two Queen

Hotel confirms: Both are 510 square feet on the rooms index, and both blurbs name the fountains. The king page adds one king bed and a maximum of three guests.

Our recommendation: This is the request for a couple or a trio who came for the fountains and do not need 626 square feet.

Ask the hotel: King or two queens, and that the category on the confirmation is Fountain View, not Premier.

Penthouse Suite in the Bellagio Tower

Hotel confirms: 1,536 square feet, one king, maximum four guests, "nestled in the Bellagio Tower," available with a Fountain View, a Strip View, or a Sunset View. Luxury reservations are listed at 844.479.4626.

Our recommendation: This is the suite to book when you want the view named before you arrive. A note on a Premier King reservation does not turn it into this choice.

Ask the hotel: Which of the three views is open, and whether you need the connecting Tower Deluxe Room for a second bedroom.

One Bedroom Lakeview, or Two Bedroom Grand Lakeview

Hotel confirms: The rooms index groups both under fountain view. The one bedroom is 2,075 square feet, one king, maximum four, "views of the lake." The two bedroom grand is 3,001 square feet, two kings, maximum six, "views of the Bellagio Fountains."

Our recommendation: Use these when the group needs a suite and the lake or the fountains are the point. Read the blurb you are buying. "Lake" and "fountains" are not the same sentence.

Resort Tower King, if the point is space

Hotel confirms: 626 square feet, one king, maximum three, panoramic views, a soaking tub plus a glass-enclosed shower.

Our recommendation: Book it for the extra 116 square feet and the tub, not because you have decided it faces the fountains. The page does not say that.

The hotel does not publish specific room numbers for these categories, so we do not name any. Ask for the category and the outlook.

Rooms to avoid and why

Premier King or Spa Premier King, if the fountains are the point

Hotel confirms: Both are 510 square feet on the rooms index. Neither blurb we read names the fountains, the Strip, or a tower.

Our recommendation: "Spa" in the name is not a published promise that the room is in the Spa Tower or that it faces Las Vegas Boulevard. The FAQ uses "Spa Tower" for accessible deluxe rooms. The Spa Premier King blurb does not.

A floor request with no category behind it

Hotel confirms: No floor chart on the room pages we opened. The 19th floor of the Bellagio Tower is the smoking exception, not a view plan.

Our recommendation: A request for "a high floor" can be honored with a Resort Tower King that never sees the fountains. Book the view in the room name first.

The 19th floor of the Bellagio Tower, if you want the non-smoking rule without the exception

Hotel confirms: Rooms and suites are non-smoking except certain accommodations in the Bellagio Tower on the 19th floor.

Ask the hotel: That your room is not one of those accommodations. The FAQ does not say the whole floor allows smoking.

How to ask

  • At booking: choose Fountain View King, Fountain View Two Queen, a lakeview suite, or the Penthouse, depending on which sentence you need. A comment on a Premier reservation is not the same thing.
  • Penthouse views: say Fountain, Strip, or Sunset. The page lists all three. It does not say every penthouse has all three.
  • By phone: the penthouse page lists luxury reservations at 844.479.4626. Accessible rooms go through the Bell Desk at 702.693.6555. The FAQ says the Spa Tower holds 22 accessible deluxe rooms and the Bellagio Tower holds 63 accessible resort rooms, with roll-in showers.
  • Arrival: the FAQ says an early check-in selection is 11:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m. and depends on availability. If the room is not ready before 3:00 p.m., luggage can sit at the Bell Desk at no charge. Dogs are not permitted. Trained service dogs are. The FAQ says emotional support animals are not service animals under its reading of the ADA and are not permitted.
  • At check-in: ask whether a fountain-facing room in the category you already booked is open before you take the key. Do not expect the desk to move you from Premier to Fountain View without a new rate.

Questions about rooms at Bellagio

Which Bellagio room faces the fountains?

The Fountain View King page says that room looks at the fountains. It is 510 square feet, one king, maximum three guests. The rooms index lists a Fountain View Two Queen at the same size, and it groups the lakeview suites with the fountain rooms. Read the suite blurb. One says lake. The grand lakeview says fountains.

Does Bellagio publish which floor to request?

Not on the room pages we opened on 20 November 2026. There is no stack chart and no room number. The FAQ's only floor is the 19th floor of the Bellagio Tower, and that sentence is about smoking accommodations, not about the fountains.

Is Resort Tower King a fountain room?

The page does not say so. It says 626 square feet, panoramic views, and a stay high above Las Vegas. Book it for space. Book Fountain View King for the show.

Which suite lists fountain, Strip, and sunset?

The Penthouse Suite. The page says it is in the Bellagio Tower and is available with a Fountain View, a Strip View, or a Sunset View. It is 1,536 square feet with one king.
