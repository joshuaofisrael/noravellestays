Best luxury & boutique hotels in Charleston, South Carolina

Charleston luxury starts with a sleep decision, not a Rainbow Row photo. The French Quarter and the City Market put dinner, King Street, and the waterfront in one walk, and they ask you to protect sleep from carriages, rooftops, and Saturday crowds. South of Broad and Broad Street put a quieter residential morning at the door, and they ask you to plan the Market as an outing. Upper King and Marion Square put shopping, a larger hotel, and a rooftop pool together, a few blocks off the loudest Market corner. Mount Pleasant puts a pool and a marina across the Ravenel Bridge. Kiawah puts the ocean and a spa a real drive from the peninsula. West Ashley and Folly Beach are useful days, and they are not where these picks sleep. Choose the neighborhood first. Best luxury neighborhoods for a splurge

Historic District / South of Broad

South of Broad is the residential tip of the peninsula: Rainbow Row, White Point Garden, the Battery seawall, and streets of historic houses that are quieter after dinner than North Market Street. It is the right morning for a luxury weekend, and it is a weak place to assume a large hotel lobby. None of the illustrated Noravelle picks sleep in the middle of those Battery blocks. John Rutledge House Inn, at 116 Broad Street, is the house scale stay on the street that names the neighborhood. The inn says the house was built in 1763 and that it has 19 guest rooms and suites. It describes breakfast, afternoon tea, and an evening pour of port, sherry, or brandy. Broad Street is the edge, not a hidden lane three blocks toward the water. You can walk south to the Battery in the morning and north to King Street for dinner. You give up a rooftop pool and a ballroom. That tradeoff is the point of a house. A small inn can sell through on a wedding weekend while The Charleston Place still shows rooms. Ask which room faces the courtyard or the piazza if a carriage route uses Broad in the morning. Walk the lower peninsula with the Charleston Historic District day plan before you decide the Battery has to be outside the bedroom window.

French Quarter and the City Market

The French Quarter is the walkable luxury core for most anniversary trips: Church Street, State Street, Queen Street, and the blocks between King Street and East Bay. The City Market runs along Market Street for several blocks of stalls, and North Market Street is where that energy meets hotel doors. Planters Inn, at 112 North Market Street, sits on the corner of Market and Meeting. The hotel says the inn dates to 1844 at that corner, that it is the only Relais & Châteaux property in South Carolina, and that Peninsula Grill is the restaurant beyond the garden courtyard. Street sound at that corner is real. Ask for a courtyard room if sleep matters as much as the address. The Spectator Hotel, at 67 State Street, is the Jazz Age boutique the hotel places about a block from the City Market, at State and Linguard. It describes personal butler service, breakfast served in the room, and The Bar at The Spectator. Its site lists check in at 4 pm and check out at noon. The Loutrel, at 61 State Street, describes 50 rooms and suites, a rooftop terrace over the French Quarter, the Veranda Lounge, a fitness studio, and complimentary breakfast. Its site lists check in at 3 pm and check out at 11 am, and valet among the amenities. French Quarter Inn, at 166 Church Street, describes complimentary breakfast in the room, the lobby, or the courtyard terrace, plus an evening wine and cheese reception. The hotel places the City Market a one minute walk away. Emeline, at 181 Church Street, is the French Quarter hotel with the poolside restaurant Little Palm. You gain the Market, King Street, and Waterfront Park on foot. You give up a Mount Pleasant lawn and a Kiawah balcony. Line the inns up with where to stay in Charleston before you lock a Market facing room.

King Street and Upper King

King Street is the shopping and restaurant spine, from the antique and design blocks near Broad Street up through the busier clothing blocks and on toward Upper King and Marion Square. The night gets quieter as you move north of the Market, and it does not go silent. Rooftop pools and lounges are part of the newer hotels. Hotel Bennett, at 404 King Street, is the large luxury hotel on that upper stretch. The hotel describes a heated rooftop pool on the 9th floor above Marion Square, open daily from 7 am to 8 pm for overnight guests, private cabanas as a separate booking, La Patisserie, and The Spa at Hotel Bennett. A pool above the square is a practical answer to a July afternoon. It is also a social roof. Ask for a room off the pool deck if the shops are the reason you came and the lounge is not. The Dewberry, at 334 Meeting Street, is the mid century hotel a short walk from the same square. The site lists Citrus Club, The Living Room, in room dining, and a spa. The building reads as a design stay, not a courtyard inn and not a beach resort. You gain King Street at the door and a bigger footprint than a 19 room house. You give up the Battery quiet and a harbor marina. The Charleston Place, at 205 Meeting Street, is the full service landmark where Meeting Street meets the Market and King Street is a block away. Use it when the weekend needs more than one room type. Use Bennett when the rooftop and Upper King set the day. Use a State Street inn when the French Quarter itself is the product.

Waterfront and Vendue Range

Vendue Range and East Bay are the harbor edge of the French Quarter. Waterfront Park, the Pineapple Fountain, and the swings along the Cooper River are the morning walk. Rooftop bars are the evening. The Vendue, at 19 Vendue Range, describes itself as an art hotel, with a large collection of work in the building and a rooftop bar. The art is the reason to choose it over a plain Market room. The roof is the reason to ask which guest room sits under it. HarbourView Inn, at 2 Vendue Range, describes on site parking, a private rooftop terrace with harbor views, continental breakfast, and an evening wine and cheese reception. On site parking is unusual in this grid and worth reading in the total, because a fee can still apply and the hotel should say so. Market Pavilion Hotel, at 225 East Bay Street, describes Grill 225 and the Pavilion Bar, a rooftop with a pool. East Bay is a lively hotel street. A cascading pool on the roof is a feature and a noise source on a warm Saturday. Ask for a room off the roof and off the busiest frontage. You gain the harbor light and a short walk to the Market. You give up South of Broad silence and a Kiawah beach. Fort Sumter is a boat trip from the harbor, not a stroll from the lobby. Build that outing on a day you are not also crossing the Ravenel Bridge for dinner.

Ansonborough

Zero George, at 0 George Street, is the courtyard hotel in Ansonborough, north of the Market and east of Meeting Street. The hotel describes four restored historic residences and two brick carriage houses around a private garden courtyard, and it dates the buildings to 1804. It describes daily breakfast, The Restaurant at Zero George, and The Caviar Bar. The hotel says the restaurant was selected for the 2025 MICHELIN Guide American South. That is the hotel’s own description of the dining room, not a score we assigned. The block is calmer than North Market Street, which is why couples choose it for a culinary weekend that still wants the peninsula on foot. George Street is not a secret forest. Carriages, a restaurant seating, and a full inn are still a downtown night. Ask which room opens to the courtyard and which one faces the street. You gain a small property and dinner on site. You give up a rooftop pool, a spa menu the size of Hotel Bennett’s, and a ballroom. A courtyard inn of this scale can sell out of the useful suite while a Meeting Street landmark still shows inventory. If the restaurant is the reason for the dates, book the table when you book the room, on the hotel’s own channels, and do not assume a lobby can absorb a walk in on a Saturday.

Wentworth Street and the College blocks

Wentworth Street sits in the middle of the peninsula, walkable to King Street and to the College of Charleston, and residential enough that it sleeps differently from the Market. Wentworth Mansion, at 149 Wentworth Street, describes a house built in 1886 with 21 guest rooms and suites, a rooftop cupola, and Circa 1886 in the original carriage house. The hotel says a stay includes breakfast at that restaurant, afternoon wine, an evening pour, and complimentary valet parking. The Woodhouse Spa is on the property. Twenty one rooms means a sold out holiday week is ordinary. The cupola is a view you climb for, not a nightclub floor, and the carriage house restaurant is still a room that can host a rehearsal dinner. Ask whether an event is on the calendar. The Restoration, at 75 Wentworth Street, is the other end of the same street, toward King. The hotel describes residential style suites, some with kitchens, breakfast baskets delivered to the room, a heated rooftop pool, and The Watch rooftop kitchen. It describes the property as Michelin recognized. Read that as the hotel’s claim, then judge the room and the roof for yourself. A rooftop restaurant above King Street is a different night from a mansion cupola. You gain space that feels more like a residence, which helps a longer stay. You give up the Broad Street house story and the harbor at the end of Vendue Range. College weekends and reunion traffic can fill these blocks even when the Market looks ordinary at noon. Tell the hotel, in writing, if sleep matters more than the roof.

Mount Pleasant

Mount Pleasant is across the Cooper River on the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge, often called the Ravenel Bridge. The bridge is the luxury trade. You get lawn, water, and a pool deck that a French Quarter lot cannot hold. You give up walking home from King Street. Rush hour stacks the bridge. So does a holiday weekend. So does the Cooper River Bridge Run, which closes the bridge on race morning. Charleston Harbor Resort & Marina, at 20 Patriots Point Road, is the resort in this set. The site describes two hotels on the property, The Beach Club and Harborside, a large pool deck, the Estuary Spa, a marina, and waterfront dining. Patriots Point looks back at the peninsula. That view is the evening. Downtown dinner is a rideshare or a car, and you should decide the return before you book a late table. Weddings use the pier and the lawn. Ask if a celebration is on the grounds for your dates. Shem Creek and the Mount Pleasant waterfront are a separate evening from the resort, still on the same side of the bridge, and still not a substitute for a State Street inn. Choose Mount Pleasant when a pool day is the trip and the Historic District is one planned outing. Choose the peninsula when the restaurant walk is the trip and the pool is optional. The Charleston destination hub is the short map of that fork before you pay for a harbor view you will cross town to escape.

Kiawah, Folly Beach, and Isle of Palms

The Sanctuary at Kiawah Island Golf Resort is the oceanfront hotel in this set. The hotel places the resort about 21 miles from Charleston, describes 255 guest rooms, a spa, and Forbes Five Star service. That last line is the hotel’s description, not a rating Noravelle invented or copied onto a card. The story page says the hotel opened in August 2004. Golf, the spa, and a balcony over the Atlantic are the reasons to sleep here. The Historic District is a day trip, and a day trip with a bridge, an interstate stretch, and island roads. Do not plan Rainbow Row at sunrise and a Kiawah dinner on the same evening you land. Folly Beach, south of the peninsula across the Ashley, is a beach day from a downtown hotel and a weak luxury base in this set. None of these picks sleep on Folly. Isle of Palms, north of Mount Pleasant, is the other beach day, closer to the Ravenel Bridge than Kiawah is and still not a French Quarter walk. We do not name a Folly or Isle of Palms hotel here because this page is not a full barrier island directory. Use those beaches when sand is the afternoon and the peninsula is still where you wake up. Use The Sanctuary when the beach is the stay. Confirm spa times and any resort gate or parking rules on the official site before you pay. We do not publish a live tee sheet.

West Ashley

West Ashley is across the Ashley River, west of the peninsula, with shopping corridors and a faster drive to some of the Ashley River historic houses than a Mount Pleasant address will give you. It is the practical side of town when a meeting or a family house is already there. It is a poor stand in for a French Quarter weekend. None of the Noravelle picks sleep in West Ashley. Treat it as a day outing from a peninsula hotel, or as a filter when you later compare a property that is not on this page. The bridges back to downtown are short on a map and slow at rush hour, and a Saturday Market evening makes the return part of the plan. You would gain easier parking than a Market corner and lose the walk that makes Charleston luxury feel different from a suburban full service hotel. If every courtyard suite on this page is gone for your dates, a West Ashley hotel can be a deliberate backup you verify yourself. It should not be the first click. Start with the official sites named here, then widen the search only if the room type you want is actually unavailable.

Near CHS

Full service hotels beside Charleston International Airport suit a dawn departure or a late landing. They are logistics for the flight, separate from a French Quarter, King Street, Mount Pleasant, or Kiawah splurge. The airport sits in North Charleston, not on the peninsula. CHS says ground transportation is outside baggage claim at the west end of the terminal. Rideshare pickup is a covered shelter. For Uber or Lyft, the airport says to follow signs to Zones 1, 2, or 3, across both roadways and to the right of the last sidewalk. Taxis use the ground transportation booth in the center median, where a starter quotes the trip. We do not publish the current taxi minimum or a rideshare estimate, because fares move. There is no downtown rail from the terminal. Hotel shuttles, when a property offers one, are listed on that hotel site, not here. This site does not publish a separate CHS airport hotel guide. Use a terminal hotel only when the flight sets the night, then move to the neighborhood that fits the rest of the trip. Sketch the arrival with flights to Charleston before you pay for a Vendue Range room you will barely use on a 6 am departure.

How to choose

Pick the job of the trip before you compare suite photos. A restaurant weekend wants a French Quarter, Ansonborough, or Market door, with a courtyard or a quieter side requested in writing. A shopping weekend wants King Street, Hotel Bennett, The Dewberry, or The Restoration. A house weekend wants Wentworth Mansion or John Rutledge House Inn. A harbor morning wants Vendue Range or East Bay, and it accepts a rooftop. A pool and marina weekend wants Mount Pleasant and the drive. A beach weekend wants Kiawah and a different clock. Small houses sell out first. John Rutledge House Inn describes 19 rooms. Wentworth Mansion describes 21. The Loutrel describes 50. The Sanctuary describes 255. The Charleston Place and Hotel Bennett are the large downtown footprints in this set, and a larger hotel can still show rooms when a courtyard suite is gone. Timing, seasonality, and event weekends

Spoleto Festival USA is the late spring arts festival that rewrites peninsula weekends. Performances spread across downtown venues, so a quiet street at noon can be a crowd at the theater door that night. The 2026 season has concluded. The festival homepage points readers to updates for 2027. An official apprenticeship page lists the 2027 festival as May 28 through June 13, 2027. Confirm that window on spoletousa.org before you hold a room, because a public calendar can move after a staffing page is posted. Book the courtyard room or the higher floor you actually want before those dates, and read the cancellation window. A 19 room inn can sell out while Hotel Bennett or The Charleston Place still shows inventory.

The Cooper River Bridge Run is the other dated disruption. Bridge Run lists race day as Saturday, April 3, 2027. The closure notes say the Ravenel Bridge closes that morning, with Mount Pleasant road closures starting overnight and early. A Patriots Point hotel and a French Quarter hotel are on opposite sides of a shut bridge for those hours. If you are sleeping in Mount Pleasant and you have a peninsula breakfast, or the reverse, move the plan off race morning. Do not treat a rideshare pin as a promise while the course is on the bridge.

Spring garden weeks and fall wedding weekends are the other inventory shocks, even without a single festival name on the calendar. Many rehearsals want the same Saturday. Holidays, including Thanksgiving week, the days around Christmas, and New Year’s, tighten the small inns first. Summers are hot and humid. A rooftop pool at Hotel Bennett, The Restoration, Market Pavilion, or The Charleston Place is a practical amenity in July and August, not a postcard extra. Plan shade, water, and a lighter first afternoon. If heat already limits your day, ask your own clinician before you stack the Battery and a long King Street walk. Atlantic hurricane season runs from June through November. We do not publish storm predictions. A flexible rate matters more than a prepaid discount if a forecast could move the trip. We do not invent nightly prices. The Charleston Area CVB is the place to confirm what is on the visitor calendar before you assume a random week is quiet.

Noise, carriages, and King Street

The Market is loud because the street is the product. Stalls, restaurants with open doors, carriage tours, and Saturday crowds stack on North Market, East Bay, and the corners around Meeting. Wedding weekends, Spoleto nights, and holiday weeks are the loudest. We do not invent decibel scores. If you want that energy for one planned evening, stay close and ask for a courtyard or a room off the street. If you want it as a visit and not as a bedroom, sleep on Wentworth, on Broad Street, in Ansonborough, or on Upper King and rideshare back.

Planters Inn has the Market at the door and a garden courtyard once you are inside. Those are both true, and the second does not cancel the first. The Spectator Hotel and The Loutrel sit about a block off the stalls on State Street, and both have their own lounges, so lobby and rooftop sound are part of the stay even if you never enter a Market restaurant. The Vendue, HarbourView Inn, and Market Pavilion add roofs. A harbor view and a quiet night are a request, not a default. Hotel Bennett’s pool deck faces Marion Square. The Restoration’s Watch faces King Street. The Dewberry is a design hotel with its own dining rooms, closer to the square than to the Battery. Wentworth Mansion is the calmer downtown house in the illustrated set, on a residential block, with a carriage house restaurant that can still host a party. John Rutledge House Inn is calmer still after dinner, with Broad Street carriages as the morning exception. Zero George sits between those poles: quieter than the Market, still a downtown restaurant hotel. Tell the hotel which side of the building you want, and keep that note with the reservation.

Getting there, parking, and rideshare

Peninsula hotels in this set lean on valet, a small lot, or a nearby city garage. Wentworth Mansion says valet is complimentary. HarbourView Inn describes on site parking. The Charleston Place offers valet at the Meeting Street entrance and, on its policies page, points self parking toward nearby city garages, including the Hasell Street Garage and Majestic Square Garage on King Street. Other inns include valet or breakfast in some rates and list them as extras in others. A spa treatment, a cabana, a second car, and a resort fee can all sit outside the headline rate. We do not publish live valet or garage prices because they change. Read the total on the official hotel site before you compare a Market inn with a Mount Pleasant resort. A car is useful for airport luggage, Kiawah, Folly, Isle of Palms, and a Mount Pleasant base. It is a burden on Market and King, where streets are narrow, event weekends fill garages, and the scenic block is not a place to circle. Leaving the car parked and using rideshare for dinner is the usual luxury pattern.

Rideshare pickup on the Market blocks is the friction point. On a busy Saturday the pin you drop on North Market may not be where a car can stop. Walk a block toward a quieter cross street, or ask the hotel which door it prefers. From CHS, use the rideshare shelter or the taxi median described above, then go to the neighborhood that matches the arrival hour. There is no train into downtown. A peninsula stay can work with walking and rideshare for the whole leisure weekend. Add a car or a scheduled ride when the plan includes the beach or Patriots Point. For the flight itself, start with flights to Charleston, then choose the neighborhood. If you are still deciding whether a coastal walking city is the right splurge at all, compare this map with Savannah luxury hotels before you pay. Savannah is a different grid, with its own squares, and it is not a substitute room on King Street.

How to shape the weekend

A clean luxury weekend has one sleep neighborhood and one intentional outing off that map. Land, check the official room location, and do not schedule the Battery, a Market dinner, a Mount Pleasant sunset, and a Kiawah hour on the same arrival evening. If you sleep in the French Quarter or on Vendue Range, walk to dinner nearby and pick one window for Upper King or the Battery. If you sleep at Wentworth Mansion, Zero George, or on Broad Street, treat the Market as a planned outing with a walk or a rideshare home. If you sleep in Mount Pleasant or at The Sanctuary, give downtown a single daytime block and eat near the hotel at night. A Bennett or Charleston Place morning can be the pool. A South of Broad morning can be White Point Garden. The Charleston weekend trip guide and the Historic District day plan carry that shape in more detail. Practical tips for a premium stay

  • Spoleto in late spring, the Bridge Run in early April, spring garden weeks, fall weddings, and holidays tighten peninsula inns first. A 19 room house can sell out while The Charleston Place or Hotel Bennett still shows rooms. Compare courtyard rooms and quieter sides early on the official hotel site.
  • Valet, parking, taxes, spa time, and cabanas change the total. Wentworth Mansion says valet is included. Other hotels list it as extra. Read inclusions on the official hotel site. We do not invent live prices.
  • Charleston summers are hot and humid, and hurricane season runs from June through November. A rooftop pool is practical in July and August at the hotels that describe one. Plan shade and a lighter first afternoon. If heat already limits your day, ask your own clinician before a long outdoor day. Prefer a flexible rate if a forecast could shift the trip.

What to know before you book

Which neighborhood fits a Charleston luxury stay?

The French Quarter and the City Market, including Planters Inn on North Market Street, The Spectator Hotel and The Loutrel on State Street, French Quarter Inn and Emeline on Church Street, and The Charleston Place on Meeting Street, suit walkable dining and King Street if you ask for a courtyard or a quieter room. Vendue Range and East Bay, including The Vendue, HarbourView Inn, and Market Pavilion Hotel, suit the harbor and a rooftop if you ask for a room off the roof. Ansonborough and Zero George suit a courtyard and dinner on the property. Upper King and Marion Square, including Hotel Bennett and The Dewberry, suit shopping and a pool or a design hotel. Wentworth Street, including Wentworth Mansion and The Restoration, suits a mansion or a suite a short walk from King. Broad Street and John Rutledge House Inn suit a house and a Battery morning. Mount Pleasant and Charleston Harbor Resort suit a pool and a marina when you accept the Ravenel Bridge. Kiawah and The Sanctuary suit the ocean and a spa when the peninsula is a planned day. West Ashley, Folly Beach, and Isle of Palms are real days out, and none of these picks sleep there. Choose the neighborhood before you compare suite photos.

Should I choose the Historic District, King Street, the French Quarter, the waterfront, or Mount Pleasant?

Choose a French Quarter or Market inn when dinner and the City Market set the trip, and you can ask for a courtyard room. Choose King Street or Upper King when shopping, a rooftop pool, or a larger hotel sets the trip. Choose Broad Street or South of Broad when you want a house scale night and the Market is a walk you plan. Choose Vendue Range or East Bay when you want the harbor at the door, and you can accept rooftop sound. Choose Mount Pleasant when the pool and the marina matter more than walking home from dinner. Choose Kiawah when the beach is the stay and Charleston is the day trip. Confirm fees and cancellation on each official site.

How loud is the City Market compared with South of Broad or Upper King?

The City Market, North Market Street, and the blocks around East Bay can carry carriage noise, street dining, and evening crowds, especially on weekends, festival nights, and wedding weekends. Planters Inn sits on the Market corner, so that sound is at the door even when the courtyard is quiet. The Spectator Hotel and The Loutrel sit on State Street about a block off the Market, and both have lounges, so lobby and rooftop sound are part of those stays. Vendue Range rooftops at The Vendue and HarbourView Inn face the harbor and still hear the quarter. Market Pavilion Hotel adds a rooftop pool on East Bay. South of Broad and the Broad Street inn are calmer after dinner. Upper King and Marion Square are busy by day and calmer than the Market after the stores close, and Hotel Bennett, The Dewberry, and The Restoration add their own roofs and dining rooms. Wentworth Mansion sits on a residential block. We do not invent noise scores. Ask the hotel when you book.

What fees should I expect, and how should cancellation work?

Valet, self parking, resort or destination fees, and taxes can change the total. A spa treatment, a rooftop cabana, breakfast, and an airport transfer are often separate charges. Wentworth Mansion says valet parking is complimentary and that breakfast is included. Other inns include breakfast or valet, and others list them as extras. The Charleston Place offers valet at the Meeting Street entrance. Read the inclusions on the official hotel site, because a headline rate is not the total. We do not post invented rates. Spoleto, the Cooper River Bridge Run, spring garden weeks, and fall wedding weekends often tighten cancellation rules. If your dates sit near an event, or a plan could shift, prefer a more flexible rate even when that total is higher.

When should I book for Spoleto, the Bridge Run, and wedding weekends?

Spoleto Festival USA is the late spring arts festival on the peninsula. The 2026 season has concluded. A festival apprenticeship page lists the 2027 festival as May 28 through June 13. Confirm those dates on the festival site before you hold a room, because public calendars can move. The Cooper River Bridge Run lists Saturday, April 3, 2027 as race day, with the Ravenel Bridge closed that morning, which changes the drive between Mount Pleasant and the peninsula. Spring garden weeks, fall wedding weekends, and holidays tighten small inns first. A 19 room house or a courtyard inn can sell out while The Charleston Place or Hotel Bennett still shows rooms. Compare courtyard rooms, higher floors, and quieter sides early on the official hotel site. Read the cancellation window before you pay. Flexible terms matter more than a prepaid discount if dates might shift. We do not invent nightly prices.

Do I need a car from CHS, or is rideshare enough?

A Historic District, French Quarter, King Street, or waterfront stay can work with walking and rideshare for dinner, the Market, and King Street. Mount Pleasant, Folly Beach, Isle of Palms, and Kiawah are easier with a car or a planned rideshare. Charleston International Airport sits in North Charleston. The airport says rideshare pickup uses a covered shelter outside baggage claim, reached by following signs to Zones 1, 2, or 3 and crossing both roadways. Taxis use the ground transportation booth in the center median outside baggage claim. There is no downtown rail from the terminal. We do not publish live traffic times or fares. Should I choose a courtyard inn or The Charleston Place?

Choose Zero George, Planters Inn, The Spectator Hotel, Wentworth Mansion, John Rutledge House Inn, or HarbourView Inn when you want a small historic property, a courtyard or a house, and fewer public spaces. Choose The Charleston Place when you want a full service Meeting Street landmark, a rooftop pool, club level, and more room types for a wedding weekend or a longer stay. The hotel describes a large event footprint, so public areas can be lively. Hotel Bennett is the other large downtown pick when you want a King Street door, a spa, and a heated rooftop pool above Marion Square. Ask which room faces inward, then confirm fees and cancellation on each official site.

How should I plan a beach or Mount Pleasant day?

Charleston Harbor Resort, at Patriots Point in Mount Pleasant, is the pool, spa, and marina base across the Ravenel Bridge. The resort describes The Beach Club and Harborside, a large pool deck, the Estuary Spa, and a marina. Downtown is a ride, and longer at rush hour and on Bridge Run morning. The Sanctuary at Kiawah Island Golf Resort is the oceanfront hotel and spa. The hotel places the resort about 21 miles from Charleston and describes 255 guest rooms. That suits a beach stay when the Historic District is one planned day, not every dinner. Folly Beach and Isle of Palms are beach days from a peninsula hotel, and none of these picks sleep on Folly. We do not publish a live spa calendar. Confirm what the hotel can arrange before you pay.
