Best luxury & boutique hotels in Kansas City, Missouri

Kansas City luxury starts with a neighborhood, not a skyline photo. The Country Club Plaza puts Spanish-inspired shops, dinner, and a fountain walk at the door, and it asks you to decide whether you want a 132 room rooftop hotel, a 126 room jazz hotel, or a much larger full service house on Ward Parkway. Downtown and Power and Light put T-Mobile Center, the convention center, and a late night within a few blocks, and they ask you to protect sleep from the street. The Crossroads puts galleries between those two maps. Crown Center puts Union Station and the National WWI Museum on the streetcar, in a large hotel rather than a Plaza boutique. Westport is a dining evening north of the Plaza, not a hotel cluster in this set. The River Market is a morning at the other end of the same free streetcar. Arrowhead and Kauffman Stadium sit together east of downtown, a ride from every pick here. The airport has no rail into the city. Choose the neighborhood first. Then open the official hotel site for the pick that fits.

Best luxury neighborhoods for a splurge

Country Club Plaza

The Plaza is the walkable luxury core when the trip is shops, a long lunch, fountains, and one intentional night out. It is a weak place to assume a downtown arena is next door. Brush Creek, Ward Parkway, and the west side along 48th Place are different blocks of the same district. The Fontaine, at 901 West 48th Place, is the west Plaza hotel. The hotel describes 132 guestrooms and suites, a rooftop pool open to guests from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., and Nine Zero One as the rooftop restaurant and bar for breakfast through dinner and weekend brunch. It calls the setting West Country Club Plaza and says there is no full service spa on site. The FAQ lists check-in at 4 p.m. and check-out at 11 a.m. A roof with cocktail service is the reason many people choose the hotel, and it is also the reason to ask for a room off the pool if sleep matters more than the view. The same FAQ lists valet at $40 per night and self parking at $35 per night, both with in and out privileges, a day or event rate, a pet fee of $75 per stay, and a $50 per night incidental hold. It says an Urban Destination Fee includes Wi-Fi, water, local rides in the hotel Tesla, a weekday lobby reception, and coffee. Read that fee on the current FAQ before you compare a headline rate. The hotel also describes a complimentary shuttle inside the immediate Plaza neighborhood, which is useful on the west side and is not a ride to Arrowhead.

The Raphael Hotel, Autograph Collection, at 325 Ward Parkway, is the smaller historic Plaza hotel in this set. The hotel says the building was constructed in 1927 as the Villa Serena Apartments, opened in 1928, and became a hotel in 1975. It describes 126 rooms, Italian Renaissance Revival architecture, and Chaz as the restaurant and lounge with nightly live jazz. One hundred twenty six rooms means a Plaza Art Fair weekend can sell the useful suite while a larger Ward Parkway hotel still shows inventory. The hotel says new ownership by Bentley Legacy Holdings and Pendant Capital has a refresh of guestrooms and public spaces on the horizon. Read that as a live hotel with work announced, not as a closure. Ask for a Plaza view only if you want the street. Ask for a quieter room if Chaz is the reason you came and the music is not the bedroom.

InterContinental Kansas City at the Plaza, at 401 Ward Parkway, is the full service Plaza flagship. An IHG fact sheet for the hotel describes 371 guest rooms, including 29 suites, and a 2017 renovation. The live hotel page describes American Slang Modern Brasserie and a seasonal pool deck, and it places Kansas City International Airport about 23 miles away. That distance is the hotel's planning note, not a promise in traffic. You gain more room types, a larger dining room, and a Plaza address that can hold a wedding or a meeting. You give up the scale of a 126 room house. Public rooms can be social on an event day. Ask which balcony faces the quiet side if the fountains are the point and a ballroom is not. The Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art and its sculpture garden sit east of the shops. From Ward Parkway that is a pleasant walk or a short ride, depending on the hour and the heat. It is not a substitute for deciding where you sleep.

Westport

Westport is the dining and nightlife district north of the Plaza, around Westport Road and Broadway. It is an evening, not a hotel cluster in this set. None of the Noravelle picks use a Westport address. The streetcar runs on Main Street to the Plaza and UMKC. It does not turn into Westport. A Plaza hotel is the right base when you want one dinner in Westport and a quieter pillow after. A downtown hotel makes that dinner a longer ride at the end of the night. We do not publish a live walk time from 48th Place to Westport Road, because the west Plaza and the Broadway strip are not the same block. If the trip is mostly Westport patios, say so when you choose between The Fontaine, The Raphael, and the InterContinental, and still plan the return. Do not assume a fountain view and a Westport midnight are the same walk.

Power and Light and the loud downtown blocks

Power and Light is the downtown entertainment district beside T-Mobile Center. Concert nights, arena crowds, and ordinary weekends can all carry sound. It is a strong base when the show is the trip. It is a weak place to assume Plaza quiet. Hotel Kansas City, at 1228 Baltimore Avenue, is the boutique in the district. Hyatt's October 2020 opening announcement placed it in The Unbound Collection by Hyatt, in the Kansas City Club building, and described 144 rooms. The hotel's own site sends bookers to that Hyatt page. Baltimore here is inside the nightlife grid, a short walk toward the Main Street streetcar and toward T-Mobile Center. Ask for a higher floor or a room off the busiest corner if you came for the building and not for the street. A 144 room hotel can still feel small on a sold out concert night.

Hotel Phillips Kansas City, Curio Collection by Hilton, at 106 West 12th Street, is the Art Deco tower on the edge of that district. A hotel fact sheet traces the opening to 1931, under Charles E. Phillips, and describes a 20 story building that once had a radio in every room. Visit KC's listing and that fact sheet both describe 216 guest rooms. The current Hilton dining page lists Tavernonna Italian Kitchen, Le Petit Chef, and P.S. Speakeasy, the basement bar in a former mail room. The hotel information page lists check-in at 4 p.m., check-out at 11 a.m., valet parking at $42, and no self parking. It says pets are not allowed. Twelfth and Baltimore is a practical downtown pin for a show, a streetcar ride, and a speakeasy that is part of the hotel. The speakeasy is the appeal. It is also a reason to ask which rooms sit over the liveliest side of 12th Street. Service animals are welcome. A pet stay is not.

Downtown full service and the convention grid

Loews Kansas City Hotel, at 1515 Wyandotte Street, is the large downtown hotel in this set. The hotel describes 800 guestrooms, a lobby bar, a restaurant, a grab and go coffee shop, an indoor lap pool, and 60,000 square feet of meeting space connected to the Kansas City Convention Center by a pedestrian bridge. It lists check-in at 4 p.m. and check-out at 11 a.m. It places the Power and Light District, T-Mobile Center, the KC Streetcar, and the Kauffman Center for the Performing Arts within walking distance. That is the right map for a convention, a performance at the Kauffman Center, or a show when you want more than one room type left. It is the wrong map if you wanted a 126 room Plaza house and did not ask. Eight hundred rooms absorb a citywide meeting that would empty The Raphael. The bridge works both ways: the convention is convenient, and a ballroom night can be the soundtrack. Ask for a higher, quieter floor on event weeks. We do not publish a Loews parking rate. Read the total on the hotel site.

Ambassador Hotel Kansas City, Autograph Collection, at 1111 Grand Boulevard, is the smaller downtown alternative to that tower. The hotel says it occupies the former Gate City National Bank, built in 1920, a neoclassical building on the National Register of Historic Places, with original marble and a newer south tower used for meetings. Lonnie's Reno Club is the jazz supper club on the lower level. The hotel lists Friday and Saturday shows from 7 p.m. to 10 p.m., with doors at 6 p.m., and says special shows are added. The American Reserve is the lobby restaurant. The hotel's own neighborhood note puts T-Mobile Center about two blocks away. A jazz club inside the hotel is a feature on those nights and a reason to ask for a room off the lower level if you want the building without the set. Grand Boulevard is downtown, not the Plaza. You can streetcar or rideshare south to Crown Center and, on the extended line, to the Plaza. You should not pretend 11th and Grand is Ward Parkway.

Library District, and the former 21c

Hotel Savoy Kansas City, Tapestry Collection by Hilton, at 219 West 9th Street, is the historic hotel in the Library District. Hilton's page describes a downtown stay walkable to major sights, less than a mile from the River Market and from T-Mobile Center, with The Savoy restaurant in the building. The hotel information page lists 120 guest rooms, check-in at 4 p.m., check-out at 11 a.m., valet parking at $39, and no self parking. It lists a non-refundable pet fee of $75, with a stated maximum weight of 70 pounds. This is the building that operated as 21c Museum Hotel Kansas City. In January 2025 the property was rebranded as Hotel Savoy under Hilton's Tapestry Collection, with Avion Hospitality managing it. The Kansas City Star reported the change on January 16, 2025. The Savoy restaurant stayed. The rotating contemporary art program that defined 21c did not. Do not book an old 21c listing as if the museum hotel were still a separate live property. Book Hotel Savoy, and ask which room faces a quieter side of 9th Street if the Library District calm is the point and a downtown weekend is not.

The Library District sits between the River Market and the louder Power and Light core. That in-between is the product. You can walk or streetcar north to City Market in the morning and still be closer to a quiet civic block than a room on Baltimore inside the entertainment district. You give up a Plaza fountain and a rooftop pool. One hundred twenty rooms is still a house that can sell out when a concert and a convention share a weekend. Union Station, the hotel says, is a short ride rather than the doorstep. Use Crown Center for the station. Use the Savoy when the historic building and the Library District are the stay.

Crossroads Arts District

The Crossroads is the gallery and studio band south of the downtown loop and north of Crown Center, with First Fridays as the monthly evening people plan around. Confirm the current Friday on the Visit KC calendar before you treat it as a guaranteed street party. Crossroads Hotel, at 2101 Central Street, is the design hotel in this set. The hotel says the building was constructed in 1911 as the Pabst Brewing Depot, and it ties the story to the neighborhood's warehouse past. It says it holds a Michelin One Key and that it was the first hotel in Missouri to receive one. That line is the hotel's claim. Central Street is a few blocks west of the Main Street streetcar, close enough to use the line and far enough that a rainy night is a walk you notice. First Friday crowds, a lobby that the hotel treats as a neighborhood room, and a design brief are the night. Ask for a quieter room if the galleries are the reason you came and a social lobby is not. This is not a Plaza fountain hotel, and it is not an 800 room convention hotel. That is why it is on the list.

The Crossroads is also the right daytime base for a museum hour at Crown Center without sleeping in a ballroom hotel, and for a streetcar hop to the River Market without sleeping in Power and Light. It is a weaker choice if the only plan is a Chiefs game and a dawn flight, because you will ride to the stadium and ride to the airport from here just as you would from the Plaza. Choose it when the art district is the trip. If every smaller room on this page is gone for a festival weekend, Loews and the Westin are the inventory, not a reason to pretend a closed name is still a hotel.

Crown Center, Union Station, and the WWI Museum

The Westin Kansas City at Crown Center, at 1 East Pershing Road, is the full service Crown Center pick. Marriott's rooms page describes 724 rooms and suites, with views toward Crown Center, downtown, Union Station, or the National WWI Museum, plus a club lounge for eligible guests. The dining page lists One East Urban Bar and Kitchen and One East Coffee Bar, and it says room service is available. Crown Center is the calmer downtown-adjacent base: the station, the museum and memorial, shops, and the streetcar stop at Union Station. It is not Power and Light, and it is not the Plaza. A large hotel still has ballrooms. Ask whether an event is in the building on your dates, and ask for a room off the busiest side if the museum is the reason you came. Families use this base because the complex is built for a day indoors. A couple who wanted Chaz and a Plaza view will feel the difference in the first evening.

Union Station is the practical south anchor of the free streetcar. From here you can ride north through the Crossroads and downtown to the River Market, or south and west, on the line that opened to the Plaza and UMKC, without starting the day in a car. The National WWI Museum and Memorial is the cultural reason to sleep on Pershing Road rather than on Ward Parkway. The Plaza is a streetcar ride, not a mistake, when you want one afternoon of shops and you want the museum at the door. Do not schedule the museum, a Westport dinner, a Power and Light concert, and a Plaza breakfast on the same arrival evening. Crown Center is the sleep. The others are outings.

River Market

The River Market and City Market are the north end of the downtown streetcar, with a morning market, cafes, and the river nearby. None of the city picks in this set use a River Market address. That is a gap in luxury inventory, not a hidden hotel we forgot. Sleep at Hotel Savoy, Hotel Phillips, Hotel Kansas City, the Ambassador, or Loews, and ride the streetcar north for the market. Sleeping farther south, at the Westin or on the Plaza, makes the market a planned ride rather than a walk before coffee. The Berkley Riverfront extension is a separate stop. The streetcar authority scheduled passenger service there for May 18, 2026, beyond the River Market end the FAQ still describes as the north end of the UMKC line. A riverfront stroll is a daytime add. It is not a reason to skip a real hotel. If you want the market at dawn and a quiet night, the Library District is the closer pillow. If you want the market and a show, accept the Power and Light walk home or the short streetcar hop.

Excelsior Springs

The Elms Hotel and Spa, at 401 Regent Street in Excelsior Springs, is the spa night outside the city. The hotel describes a historic mineral-springs town stay, a grotto, a 28,000 square foot spa, a seasonal outdoor pool, and an indoor lap pool. It lists Three Owls, The Tavern, and Cafe Soterian. The FAQ says check-in begins at 4 p.m. and check-out is at noon, that spa access is not included and should be reserved ahead, and that the spa and grotto are for guests 18 and older. It says self parking is complimentary and lists valet at $20 for daily use and $30 overnight. It describes MCI as about 30 to 40 minutes by car. Dogs are allowed in designated rooms, with fees and restrictions the hotel says apply. This is a real luxury stay. It is not a Plaza walk, not a streetcar evening, and not a base for T-Mobile Center. Use it when the spa is the trip. Use the Plaza or downtown when the city is the trip. Do not book The Elms for a one night concert and expect to stroll to dinner on Ward Parkway.

Near MCI

Full service hotels beside Kansas City International Airport suit a dawn departure or a late landing. They are logistics for the flight, separate from a Plaza, Crossroads, Power and Light, or Crown Center splurge. None of the Noravelle picks sleep in an airport corridor. The airport's getting there page says drop-off is on the upper departures curb and pickup is on the lower arrivals curb. For app-based rideshare it says to wait until you have your bags and are at the arrivals curb, then follow signs to the arrivals commercial curb and the purple signposts marked 2K-N. There is no downtown rail from the terminal. The Fontaine's FAQ describes the drive as about 25 to 30 minutes. InterContinental's page describes about 23 miles from the Plaza hotel. Visit KC's listing for Hotel Phillips describes 19 miles. Those figures do not match because they measure different things from different doors, and none of them is a live traffic time. We do not publish a taxi fare. Use an airport hotel only when the flight sets the night, then move to the neighborhood that fits the rest of the trip. A Plaza shuttle, where The Fontaine describes one, covers the Plaza neighborhood. It does not cover the terminal.

How to choose

Pick the job of the trip before you compare suite photos. A shop and dinner weekend wants the Plaza: The Fontaine if you want the roof, The Raphael if you want the 1920s house and jazz at Chaz, the InterContinental if you want a larger full service floorplate on Ward Parkway. A gallery weekend wants the Crossroads Hotel. A concert at T-Mobile Center wants Hotel Kansas City or Hotel Phillips, with a higher floor requested in writing. A convention or a Kauffman Center night wants Loews. A historic downtown hotel with its own jazz club wants the Ambassador. A Library District building, knowing it is no longer 21c, wants Hotel Savoy. A museum and station day wants the Westin at Crown Center. A spa day outside the city wants The Elms, and it wants a car. A stadium day wants a ride to the Truman Sports Complex from whichever of those you already chose. It does not want a pretend walk.

Smaller hotels sell out first. The Fontaine describes 132 rooms. The Raphael describes 126. Hotel Savoy's page lists 120. Hyatt's opening announcement for Hotel Kansas City described 144. Visit KC and a hotel fact sheet describe 216 at Hotel Phillips. Loews describes 800 guestrooms. The Westin's rooms page describes 724. An IHG fact sheet describes 371 at the InterContinental, including 29 suites. A larger hotel can still show rooms when a Plaza suite is gone. The table is an editorial fit guide, not a ranking and not a price list. We do not invent nightly rates. Open the official site named in the last column before you pay. Read how we pick luxury hotels if you want the rules behind that short list, and how pricing works for why the compare buttons on this page stay closed.

Timing, seasonality, and event weekends

Chiefs weekends and Royals homestands are the inventory shock with a map attached. GEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium and Kauffman Stadium share the Truman Sports Complex east of downtown. Every hotel on this page is a ride away. The streetcar does not go there. The Royals ballpark guide says rideshare services use Lot A, with pregame drop-off at the stadium gates and postgame pickup in that lot. Confirm the current Chiefs arrival plan on the team site before a game day, because a garage and a rideshare pin move. Downtown still fills up, because people gather in Power and Light and because the useful room near the streetcar sells through. A 120 room Library District hotel can disappear while Loews still shows rooms. Book the room type you actually want, and read the cancellation window. We do not invent a season schedule or a nightly rate.

T-Mobile Center is the downtown version of the same problem. Hotel Phillips, Hotel Kansas City, Loews, and the Ambassador are the walks. A concert stacks the same weekend as a convention more often than a quiet Tuesday. The Kansas City Convention Center is attached to Loews by the hotel's pedestrian bridge, so a citywide show in the building is not a rumor. Ask. The Plaza Art Fair is ordinarily a late September weekend that fills Ward Parkway and the west Plaza. Confirm the next dates on Visit KC, because a public calendar can still move. First Fridays in the Crossroads are monthly and smaller. They change the block around Central Street. They do not rewrite the whole city the way a stadium weekend does.

Summers are hot and humid. A rooftop pool at The Fontaine, the seasonal pool deck the InterContinental describes, or the indoor lap pool at Loews is a practical amenity in July and August, not a postcard extra. The Elms describes a grotto and pools if the trip is the spa, and it says that access is booked separately. Plan shade, water, and a lighter first afternoon between the Plaza and the Nelson-Atkins. If heat already limits your day, ask your own clinician before you stack a long outdoor walk. Winters are a cold walk. Ice changes the case for a car between the Plaza and dinner. We do not publish a forecast. Spring storm season is a reason to prefer a flexible rate if a warning could move the trip, not a reason to skip a calm week. A prepaid discount is the wrong tool when the dates are tied to a game that you might not attend.

Noise, arenas, and rooftop pools

Power and Light is loud because the district is the product. Arenas, open doors, and weekend crowds stack on the entertainment blocks and leak onto 12th Street and Baltimore. T-Mobile Center nights are the loudest downtown version. We do not invent decibel scores. If you want that energy for one planned evening, stay at Hotel Kansas City or Hotel Phillips and ask for a higher floor. If you want the show as an outing and not as a bedroom, sleep on the Plaza, in the Crossroads, or at Crown Center, and rideshare back. Hotel Savoy is the in-between: closer to the River Market than to the middle of Power and Light, and still a downtown weekend.

The Fontaine has a rooftop pool and Nine Zero One until 10 p.m. for pool guests. Both are the appeal. The Raphael has nightly jazz at Chaz, which is the hotel's description, so a room over the lounge is a request you should make if you do not want it. The Ambassador has Lonnie's Reno Club on the lower level on the Friday and Saturday window the hotel lists, plus added shows. Loews has the convention bridge and an indoor pool. The Westin has ballrooms beside a museum. The InterContinental has a seasonal deck and event floors on the Plaza. Hotel Phillips has a speakeasy in the basement. Crossroads Hotel has a social lobby in a gallery district. Tell the hotel which side you want, and keep that note with the reservation. A phone request that never lands in the booking record is not a plan.

Getting there, parking, and the streetcar

Parking is a hotel-by-hotel number, not a citywide one. The Fontaine's FAQ lists valet at $40 per night and self parking at $35 per night, both with unlimited in and out, plus day or event parking. Hotel Phillips lists valet at $42 and says self parking is not available. Hotel Savoy lists valet at $39 and says self parking is not available. The Elms says self parking is complimentary and lists valet at $20 for the day and $30 overnight. A destination fee, a pet fee, a cabana, and a second car can all sit outside the headline rate. The Fontaine's FAQ is the place to read what its Urban Destination Fee includes before you compare that rooftop hotel with a Ward Parkway house that may structure fees differently. We do not publish a valet price for Loews, the InterContinental, The Raphael, the Ambassador, Hotel Kansas City, the Crossroads Hotel, or the Westin, because those pages did not give a figure we are willing to freeze here. Read the total on each official site. A car is useful for airport luggage, a stadium night, Westport, and Excelsior Springs. It is a burden downtown on a concert weekend, when garages fill and the scenic block is not a place to circle. Leaving the car parked, and using the streetcar, is the usual luxury pattern inside the city.

The KC Streetcar Authority says the ride is free. The FAQ describes service from UMKC to the River Market. Published hours are 5 a.m. to midnight Monday through Thursday and Sunday, and 5 a.m. to 1 a.m. on Friday and Saturday. The Main Street extension, which reached the Country Club Plaza and UMKC, opened on October 24, 2025. The authority's riverfront page scheduled passenger service on the Berkley Riverfront extension for May 18, 2026. The line connects the River Market, downtown, the Crossroads, Union Station and Crown Center, and the Plaza. It does not serve MCI, Westport, or the Truman Sports Complex. From MCI, use the arrivals commercial curb and the purple 2K-N signs described above, then go to the neighborhood that matches the arrival hour. There is no train into downtown. A Plaza, Crossroads, or downtown stay can work with the streetcar and rideshare for the whole leisure weekend. Add a car when the plan needs the stadium, Westport, or The Elms. For a mountain-city weekend with a station hotel and a different airport train, compare Denver luxury hotels before you pay. Denver is not a substitute room on Ward Parkway. If the weekend is a shorter Midwest gallery city, Columbus luxury hotels are the other map. If the night is a downtown music strip rather than a jazz room and a Plaza walk, Nashville luxury hotels are the comparison.

How to shape the weekend

A clean luxury weekend has one sleep neighborhood and one intentional outing off that map. Land, check the official room location, and do not schedule the Plaza, Westport, the Crossroads, and a Power and Light show on the same arrival evening. If you sleep at The Fontaine, The Raphael, or the InterContinental, walk to dinner nearby and pick one window for the Nelson-Atkins or for Westport. If you sleep at Hotel Kansas City, Hotel Phillips, or Loews, treat the Plaza as a streetcar outing and ask for a higher floor before the concert. If you sleep at the Westin, give the museum and Union Station the morning, and ride the streetcar to dinner. If you sleep at Hotel Savoy or the Ambassador, keep the River Market as a morning and the jazz club as the night you already chose. If you sleep at the Crossroads Hotel, let First Friday be the evening when it falls on your dates, and use the streetcar rather than adding a third neighborhood. The Elms is its own weekend. Do not bolt it onto a one night arena trip. There is no separate Kansas City where-to-stay guide or day plan on this site yet. This page is that guide. If the dates are still loose, read how pricing works before you treat a prepaid game-day rate as the careful choice. Questions about a listing can go through contact.

What to know before you book

Which neighborhood fits a Kansas City luxury stay?

Country Club Plaza hotels, including The Fontaine at 901 West 48th Place, The Raphael Hotel at 325 Ward Parkway, and InterContinental Kansas City at the Plaza at 401 Ward Parkway, suit walkable shopping and dinner. The Crossroads and the Crossroads Hotel at 2101 Central Street suit galleries and First Fridays. Power and Light and the blocks beside it, including Hotel Kansas City at 1228 Baltimore Avenue and Hotel Phillips at 106 West 12th Street, suit concerts at T-Mobile Center and a louder downtown night. Loews Kansas City Hotel at 1515 Wyandotte Street suits the convention center, which it connects to by a pedestrian bridge. The Ambassador Hotel at 1111 Grand Boulevard suits a smaller downtown hotel with a jazz club. Hotel Savoy at 219 West 9th Street, the former 21c Museum Hotel, suits the Library District between the River Market and the arena. The Westin Kansas City at Crown Center at 1 East Pershing Road suits Union Station and the National WWI Museum. Westport and the River Market are real evenings and mornings, and none of the city picks sleep there. The Elms Hotel and Spa in Excelsior Springs is a spa night outside the city, not a Plaza walk. Airport hotels beside MCI are logistics. Choose the neighborhood before you compare suite photos.

Should I choose the Country Club Plaza, the Crossroads, Power and Light, or Crown Center?

Choose the Plaza when the shops, a rooftop or a jazz room, and a calmer evening than the arena are the point. Choose the Crossroads when galleries and a design hotel matter more than a Plaza fountain. Choose Power and Light or the adjacent downtown blocks when T-Mobile Center, a convention, or a late downtown dinner is the center of gravity, and ask for a higher floor. Choose Crown Center when Union Station, the National WWI Museum, and a large full service hotel fit better than a boutique on the Plaza. Westport is an outing from the Plaza. The River Market is a morning on the streetcar. Confirm fees and cancellation on each official site.

How loud is Power and Light compared with the Plaza?

Power and Light, the blocks around T-Mobile Center, and downtown streets can carry sound late on concert nights, convention weekends, and busy Fridays. Hotel Kansas City sits in that district at 1228 Baltimore Avenue. Hotel Phillips sits on 12th Street, with Tavernonna and a basement speakeasy in the building. Loews Kansas City Hotel is a short walk from the district and from the convention center. The Ambassador Hotel describes T-Mobile Center as about two blocks away, and Lonnie's Reno Club is a jazz room inside the hotel on Friday and Saturday nights. The Plaza is usually the calmer leisure base. The Fontaine has a rooftop pool and Nine Zero One, so the roof can be social even when the street is quiet. The Raphael describes nightly live jazz at Chaz. The Crossroads is livelier on First Fridays and calmer on an ordinary Tuesday if you ask for a quieter room. Crown Center is calmer than Power and Light, and a ballroom event inside a large hotel is its own night. We do not invent noise scores. Ask the hotel when you book.

What fees should I expect, and how should cancellation work?

Valet, self parking, destination or amenity fees, pet fees, and taxes can change the total. The Fontaine's FAQ lists valet at $40 per night and self parking at $35 per night, both with in and out privileges, plus a day or event rate, a pet fee of $75 per stay, and a $50 per night incidental hold. That FAQ says an Urban Destination Fee includes Wi-Fi, water, local rides in the hotel Tesla, a weekday lobby reception, and coffee. Hotel Phillips lists valet at $42 and says self parking is not available. The hotel information page says pets are not allowed. Hotel Savoy lists valet at $39, says self parking is not available, and lists a non-refundable pet fee of $75. The Elms Hotel and Spa says self parking is complimentary and lists valet at $20 for daily use and $30 overnight, and it says spa access is not included. Read the inclusions on the official hotel site, because a headline rate is not the total and a published fee can change. We do not post invented nightly room rates. Chiefs weekends, Royals homestands, Plaza Art Fair, and large conventions often tighten cancellation rules. If your dates sit near an event, prefer a more flexible rate even when that total is higher.

When should I book for Chiefs games, Royals games, and downtown events?

GEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium and Kauffman Stadium share the Truman Sports Complex east of downtown. They are a ride from every hotel in this set. The streetcar does not go there. The Royals ballpark guide says rideshare uses Lot A. T-Mobile Center is downtown, a walk from Hotel Phillips, Hotel Kansas City, Loews Kansas City Hotel, and the Ambassador Hotel. The Kansas City Convention Center connects to Loews by a pedestrian bridge, so a citywide convention tightens that hotel and the blocks around it. The Plaza Art Fair is a late September weekend that fills Plaza hotels. Confirm the next dates on the visitor calendar, because a published weekend can still move. The Fontaine describes 132 rooms, The Raphael describes 126, and Hotel Savoy's information page lists 120 guest rooms. Those houses can sell out while Loews, which describes 800 guestrooms, or the Westin, whose rooms page describes 724 rooms and suites, still shows inventory. Compare the room type you actually want early on the official hotel site. Read the cancellation window before you pay. We do not invent nightly prices or a team schedule.

Do I need a car from MCI, or is the streetcar enough?

A Plaza, Crossroads, downtown, or Crown Center stay can work with the streetcar and rideshare for dinner, museums, and the River Market. The KC Streetcar Authority says the ride is free and describes service from UMKC to the River Market. Published hours run from 5 a.m. to midnight Monday through Thursday and Sunday, and from 5 a.m. to 1 a.m. on Friday and Saturday. The Main Street extension to the Plaza and UMKC opened on October 24, 2025. The authority scheduled passenger service on the Berkley Riverfront extension for May 18, 2026. The streetcar does not serve Kansas City International Airport, Westport, or the Truman Sports Complex. There is no downtown rail from MCI. The airport says ride app pickups use the arrivals commercial curb at the purple signposts marked 2K-N, and it says to request the ride after you have your bags. Drop-off is on the upper departures curb. The Fontaine's FAQ describes the drive as about 25 to 30 minutes. InterContinental's page describes the airport as about 23 miles from the hotel. Visit KC's listing for Hotel Phillips describes the airport as 19 miles away. Those are planning notes, not a promise for your arrival hour. A car helps with airport luggage, a stadium night, Westport, or The Elms in Excelsior Springs, and it is a burden to park downtown on an event night. We do not publish live traffic times or a taxi fare. Book on the official hotel site when the hotel, room type, and cancellation terms already fit. Should I choose a Plaza boutique or a downtown full service hotel?

Choose The Fontaine or The Raphael when you want a smaller Plaza hotel, shops at the door, and fewer meeting floors. Choose InterContinental Kansas City at the Plaza when you want a larger full service hotel on the Plaza, with American Slang and a seasonal pool deck, and you can ask for a quieter room on a wedding weekend. Choose Hotel Phillips, the Ambassador Hotel, Hotel Kansas City, or Hotel Savoy when you want a historic downtown building rather than a Plaza fountain. Choose Loews Kansas City Hotel or The Westin Kansas City at Crown Center when you want more room types, a pool, and a convention or museum base. Southmoreland on the Plaza is open as a self-service house, not as a staffed luxury hotel, and it is not one of these picks. Public areas at a conference or jazz-club hotel can be lively. Ask which room faces inward, then confirm fees and cancellation on each official site.

What happened to 21c Museum Hotel and other older Kansas City names?

21c Museum Hotel Kansas City is not a separate hotel you can book under that name. In January 2025 the property at 219 West 9th Street reopened as Hotel Savoy, Tapestry Collection by Hilton. The Savoy restaurant remains in the building. The contemporary art program that defined 21c does not. Southmoreland on the Plaza said that as of January 1, 2022 it would run as a self-service hotel and no longer as a bed and breakfast. The house is still open on that model, near the Plaza on East 46th Street. It is a different product from The Fontaine or The Raphael. The Fontaine, Hotel Phillips, InterContinental Kansas City at the Plaza, Loews Kansas City Hotel, and the Crossroads Hotel are open under those names. Confirm the current official site before you pay, and do not treat an old 21c listing as a live museum hotel.
