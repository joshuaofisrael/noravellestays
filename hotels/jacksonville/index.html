Best luxury and boutique hotels in Jacksonville, Florida

Jacksonville luxury starts with a shore, not a skyline photo. Ponte Vedra Beach holds two different resorts on one Atlantic stretch: a historic inn campus at 200 Ponte Vedra Boulevard and a 66 room Mediterranean lodge at 607 Ponte Vedra Boulevard. TPC Sawgrass is the golf map next door, and the beach from that resort is a shuttle, not the lobby. Atlantic Beach is a smaller oceanfront hotel and an 11 room inn, not a copy of Ponte Vedra. Amelia Island is a third trip, at Fernandina Beach, with an Omni resort and a Ritz-Carlton that print different airport distances and different parking rules. Downtown is the St. Johns River, the arena, and the stadium. LaVilla is a 13 apartment aparthotel on West Beaver Street. Riverside and San Marco are where you go for dinner. They are not hotel addresses on this list. Jacksonville International Airport has no train into downtown. Choose the neighborhood first. Then open the official hotel site for the pick that fits. The short map back to this page is the Jacksonville destination hub.

Best luxury neighborhoods for a splurge

Ponte Vedra Beach

Ponte Vedra Beach is the classic Northeast Florida leisure shore for this guide. It is not downtown, it is not Jacksonville Beach, and it is not Amelia Island. Two resorts share a FAQ and a beach, and they are easy to book as if they were one building. They are not. Visit Jacksonville is the wide visitor map. The hotel pages below are the sleep decision.

Ponte Vedra Inn and Club, at 200 Ponte Vedra Boulevard, Ponte Vedra Beach, Florida 32082, is the larger historic house. The inn page says it has set a standard since 1928 and calls the resort AAA Five Diamond. That diamond line is the hotel's claim. We do not audit awards, and we do not copy a diamond onto the card below. The rooms page says 262 rooms and suites sit in ten low rise Florida style buildings. Categories it names include Superior Oceanfront, Superior Beachside, Preferred Oceanfront, Preferred Beachside, Historic Inn rooms above the Great Lounge, Island View rooms toward the lagoons and the 9th island hole, Ocean Suites with a fireplace, a penthouse with private elevator access, and presidential suites. Some categories say they sleep up to five. Superior Beachside and a few others say up to three. Read the room you are actually buying. Reservations are 888 839 9145. Other calls are 904 285 1111.

The Lodge and Club, at 607 Ponte Vedra Boulevard, is the smaller sister. The lodge page calls it AAA Four Diamond, dates the debut to 1989, and describes Mediterranean architecture, palms, and dunes between Jacksonville and St. Augustine. It says the lodge has 66 designer rooms and suites and that each has beach and ocean views, with fireplaces in many rooms and kitchenettes in some. The rooms page repeats the count of 66. Sixty six rooms is a different inventory problem from 262 at the inn, and a different problem again from an Amelia Island tower. A tournament weekend can take the lodge room you wanted while the inn still shows space. Reservations are 800 243 4304. Other calls are 904 273 9500.

The shared FAQ is where the rules live, and it covers both houses. Guaranteed check in is 4 p.m. Check out is 11 a.m. Early arrival is not guaranteed. Late checkout is not granted until the day of departure. Guests may keep using the resort that day, with luggage storage and a place to change. Reservations must be cancelled seven days in advance for a deposit refund. Holidays and special events may use a different rule. Pets are not allowed. Beach chairs and umbrellas are for rent, not included in the rate. Breakfast is not included unless you add the bed and breakfast package. You may bring wine for a $35 corkage fee. The resort fee paragraph lists Wi Fi, twice daily housekeeping, in room coffee and tea, fitness center access and most fitness classes, an e press reader, self parking, and complimentary valet. It does not print a dollar amount. We do not invent one. A complimentary shuttle runs between the two resorts. The concierge can arrange a car or a limousine for a fee. That shuttle is not an airport shuttle, and the FAQ does not hand you a printed list of golf, spa, and dining privileges that automatically cross from one reservation to the other. The accessibility answer says amenities and dining around both resorts can be enjoyed by all. Read that as the hotel's access sentence, then ask what your specific rate includes at the other house before you pay.

Dining rules are part of the noise and the dress, not a footnote. At the inn, reservations are strongly recommended for the Inn Dining Room and the Golf Club Dining Room. Walk in service is the Sandpiper food truck, the snack bar, and the tavern. The Inn Dining Room is Resort Casual at breakfast and Smart Casual in the evening. The Golf Club Dining Room is Resort Casual. The FAQ says ripped jeans, T shirts, flip flops, beachwear, and athletic clothes are not permitted in those rooms, and that gentlemen wear collared shirts, long pants, and closed toe shoes. Beachside venues are more relaxed, and swimwear must be covered. At the lodge, the FAQ says dining is Resort Casual except at the grille, which is Smart Casual at dinner, with the same collared shirt rule. It spells the restaurant Sea View Grille in one sentence and Seaview Grille in another. Confirm the name when you reserve. Walk ins are welcome at the lounge and at Oasis. The Barefoot Bistro is the casual pool and fitness outlet. A wedding in the ballroom can fill a 66 room house even when the beach looks empty.

Amelia Island

Amelia Island is a barrier island north of Jacksonville, in Fernandina Beach, not a neighborhood you walk from the Hyatt. It is the right luxury core when the beach, a spa, golf, and a resort campus are the whole trip. It is a weak place to assume a downtown arena is downstairs. Two official resorts sit on this island and they do not share a fee table.
