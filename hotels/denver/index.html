Luxury hotels near downtown Denver

By Noravelle Stays Editorial. Last updated 26 Oct 2026.

Luxury hotels near downtown Denver in 2026 start at Union Station, in LoDo, in the Theatre District, and at the Brown Palace block. Open The Crawford Hotel at 1701 Wynkoop Street when you want to step off the airport train inside Union Station. Open The Oxford Hotel at 1600 17th Street for a historic house beside that station. Open The Maven Hotel at 1850 Wazee Street in Dairy Block, a block from Coors Field, when you want a lifestyle hotel in LoDo. Open Four Seasons Hotel Denver at 1111 14th Street for a full service tower across from the performing arts complex. Open The Brown Palace Hotel and Spa at 321 17th Street for the 1892 atrium. A luxury boutique hotel in Denver that is not on that downtown walk is a different decision. Halcyon Hotel Cherry Creek and Hotel Clio are the Cherry Creek pair. The ART, a hotel, is the Golden Triangle stay. The Ramble Hotel is the RiNo lifestyle hotel. Lifestyle hotels Denver searches usually mean The Maven, Halcyon, Hotel Clio, the ART, and The Ramble. Cherry Creek and RiNo are a ride from the airport train. Flight planning is on flights to Denver. Choose the neighborhood first. Then open the official hotel site. Best luxury neighborhoods for a splurge

LoDo and Union Station

Lower Downtown is the default base when restaurants, the ballpark, and the airport train set the day and you can protect sleep. It is not one hotel. The Crawford Hotel is inside Denver Union Station at 1701 Wynkoop Street, Denver, CO 80202. The phone on the site is 720 460 3700. The FAQ says the hotel offers 112 rooms, lofts, and suites. Check in is from 4:00 p.m. Check out is by 11:00 a.m. The front desk is in the Crawford Parlour, off the Great Hall, with a private elevator. The location page puts that lobby on the northwest side of the Great Hall. Valet is $68 a night at the Wynkoop Street entrance on the north side. Dogs up to 60 pounds are welcome. The FAQ says a $50 a night pet fee. The pet page says $50 a night per pet. Service animals are not charged. We did not read a cancellation window on the pages we opened, so ask before you pay a prepaid rate. The rooms page also lists access to The Oxford Club gym and fitness classes. Ask what a Crawford reservation actually includes there. A courtesy car covers a two mile radius from 7:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. That car is not an airport shuttle. The same location page describes the airport train as about 37 minutes, about 23 miles, with six stops between the airport and the station, at a cost it prints as $10.

The Oxford Hotel, at 1600 17th Street, Denver, CO 80202, is the historic house in the same district, not a second name for the Crawford. The homepage dates the opening to 1891 and describes the Oxford Club, Spa and Salon on the property, plus the Cruise Room and Urban Farmer. The FAQ says the hotel has 80 unique classic rooms. Check in is after 4 p.m. Check out is at 12 p.m. You need a credit card and a photo identification. Cancel by 4:00 p.m. Mountain Time two days before arrival or the fee is one night of room and tax. Overnight valet is $62 a vehicle. The destination amenity fee is $30 plus tax. The inclusions we read include high speed Wi Fi, two bottled waters a day on request, coffee or tea in the lobby or the room on request, a $10 credit each day to Urban Farmer or the Cruise Room, and 10 percent off spa and salon services at the club. Dogs are welcome at a one time $50 fee, with no weight limit. Other pets are not permitted. Service animals are outside that fee. The hotel is nonsmoking, and the FAQ says a $500 fee if smoking is detected. The main line on the homepage is 833 524 0368. The pet answer also prints 303 628 5400. LoDo evenings still sit outside the door. Ask for a room off the busier corner when sleep matters more than the street.

The Maven Hotel is the Dairy Block door, at 1850 Wazee Street, Denver, CO 80202, between 18th and 19th Streets. The entrance is on Wazee next to the Kachina Cantina patio. The phone is 720 460 2727. Reservations are 855 600 8140. The stay page says each of 172 loft style rooms is part of the count, with check in at 4 p.m. and check out at 11 a.m. The FAQ repeats 172 rooms and says guests must be 21 or older. The hotel authorizes room and tax plus $100 a night for incidentals and does not take cash at check in. Cancel 48 hours before arrival or the fee is one night of room and tax. The government rate note is tighter: cancel by 4 p.m. two days before arrival. Valet is $59 a car a day. The destination amenity fee is $27 plus tax a day. Dogs are $50 a pet a night, limit two, with a waiver. Service animals are complimentary. A smoking fee of $420 applies if smoking is detected. The FAQ says the hotel is two blocks from Union Station and a block from Coors Field. It also prints the airport train at $10.50 a ride and says trains leave every 15 minutes. RTD prints an Airport Day Pass at $10. Use the RTD page, and treat $10.50 as the hotel line, not the fare table. The Dairy Block fitness center is described as 4,500 square feet and up, with a key from the desk. Maven guests can receive 15 percent off a meal at Kachina Cantina. That discount is the hotel claim. We do not audit it.

Theatre District

Four Seasons Hotel Denver is the full service tower, at 1111 14th Street, Denver, Colorado 80202. The phone is 303 389 3000. The location page says the hotel is in the Theatre District, across the street from the Denver Performing Arts Complex, and three blocks from the Colorado Convention Center, with a short walk to the 16th Street Mall and Larimer Square. The accommodations page says the hotel offers 218 rooms and 21 suites in a 45 story tower and calls the building the fourth tallest in the city. That height claim is the hotel line. We do not audit it. The same page says check in is 3:00 p.m. and check out is 12:00 p.m. That check in is an hour earlier than the 4:00 p.m. line at the Crawford, the Oxford, Halcyon, the Maven, the Brown Palace, Hotel Clio, and the ART. If you land at noon and want the room immediately, the page says to book the night before. Bags can be stored, and the fitness room can be used, if you do not. Deluxe mountain view rooms are described on upper floors. The one bedroom presidential suite is listed at 2,235 square feet with a private sauna. Valet is $65 a day with unlimited in and out. The accessibility page says the hotel is valet only. Up to two pets are welcome per suite. No fee and no weight limit are printed on that page. Read the word suite before you assume a standard room. We did not read a destination fee or a cancellation window, so we do not publish either.

The amenities page describes a heated outdoor pool on a terrace, a spa, a 24 hour fitness centre looking onto that terrace, 24 hour in room dining, and a complimentary car within a 2 mile downtown radius. The same sentence prints 5 kilometres in parentheses. Two miles and five kilometres are not the same distance. Ask which radius the car actually serves, including whether Cherry Creek is inside it. The homepage calls the pool a heated rooftop pool terrace and says the Denver Center for the Performing Arts is a short block away. The location page says across the street from the Denver Performing Arts Complex. Both lines are on the official site. Use them as the hotel wording, then look at the door. Denver International Airport is listed at 27 miles and 20 to 40 minutes by car. A transfer can be arranged with the concierge. That is not a printed shuttle timetable. EDGE Restaurant and Bar is the dining room named on the homepage.

Downtown and the Brown Palace

The Brown Palace Hotel and Spa is the historic downtown landmark at 321 17th Street, Denver, CO 80202. The phone is 303 297 3111. It is an Autograph Collection hotel. It is not in Cherry Creek, and it is not inside Union Station. The FAQ says the hotel opened in 1892, designed by Frank Edbrooke, on a triangular plot at Broadway, Tremont Place, and 17th Street. It describes an eight story atrium with a stained glass skylight, afternoon tea in that atrium year round, Ship Tavern since 1934, and Palace Arms for dinner Tuesday through Saturday. Check in begins at 4:00 p.m. Check out is at 11:00 a.m. Early check in is not guaranteed. Late check out may cost an extra fee and depends on occupancy. Most reservations allow cancellation up to 48 hours before arrival unless the rate says otherwise. The exact rule is on the confirmation. We did not read a room count, so we do not publish one.

Parking at the Brown Palace disagrees with itself, which is why the number belongs in the decision. One FAQ answer says valet is $63 a night and that there is no self parking on the property. Another answer says valet is $66 a night, daily parking under 8 hours is $34, daily parking over 8 hours is $51, and a restaurant validation is a flat $18. The afternoon tea answer says valet is $15. We are not choosing $63 over $66 or over $15. Read the line attached to the rate you are buying. There is no electric vehicle charging on the property. Dogs are welcome in select rooms for an additional fee that the FAQ does not print. Service animals are always accommodated. The spa asks for 24 hours notice or you may be charged for the treatment. Afternoon tea asks for 24 hours notice on a change, and a card may be required in peak season. Holiday tea runs from early November through the end of January on the FAQ, with reservations opening on 1 June. Steer Day, which the hotel ties to the National Western Stock Show, is described as a January atrium visit by the Grand Champion Steer. If your dates sit in that week, ask whether the atrium is the quiet room you wanted.

Cherry Creek

Cherry Creek North is shopping, restaurants, and a residential evening that stays calmer than Wynkoop Street. Two hotels on this list sleep here, and they are easy to book as if they were one building. They are not. VISIT DENVER is the wide visitor map. The hotel pages below are the sleep decision.

Halcyon Hotel Cherry Creek, at 245 Columbine Street, Denver, CO 80206, sits between 2nd and 3rd Avenues. The main phone is 720 772 5000. Reservations are 855 203 0807. The FAQ says the hotel has 154 total guest rooms and 13 suites. We are not adding those figures into a total the page did not print. Check in is after 4 p.m. Check out is 12 p.m. Guests must be 21 or older if they are checking in without a registered adult. You check in at the Kitchen Counter. Cancel 72 hours before arrival or the charge is one night of room and tax. If you change the dates after the scheduled arrival, the FAQ says you will be charged 100 percent of the original stay. A 2.36 percent credit card recovery fee is added to the bill and assessed on the total charges. Cherry Creek North adds a tourism fee of 1 percent of the room charge. The destination amenity fee is $35 a night on the FAQ. The amenities page says $35 plus tax per room per day. Inclusions we read include Wi Fi, the fitness center, Kitchen Counter coffee around the clock, fruit and baked treats, a social hour of house wine and beer at Local Jones from 2:30 p.m. to 4:30 p.m., the Gear Garage, the rooftop pool deck, and electric vehicle chargers for guests using valet. The FAQ says the Gear Garage is complimentary for guests and lists scooters, electric bikes, cruiser bikes, longboards, and cameras. It spells the scooter brand Piaggo. Confirm the list when you arrive. A courtesy car runs within 3 miles, first come, from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Valet is $20 for up to 4 hours, $28 for 4 to 8 hours, and $68 for 8 to 24 hours. The pet page says $150 per pet, up to 70 pounds, two pets a room, and no cats. The FAQ says 10 percent of every monthly pet fee is donated to True Companions Animal Shelter and Clinic. Ask whether the $150 is the fee that sentence describes. The pool is on the roof, heated, and open year round from 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., with downtown and mountain views. The rooftop page also describes a hot tub, cabanas, and a bar. The FAQ names the rooftop bar Rare Bird. The hotel lists the airport at 25 miles. Downtown museums are a ride. That is the point of this neighborhood.

Hotel Clio, a Luxury Collection hotel, is the other Cherry Creek door, at 150 Clayton Lane, Denver, Colorado 80206. The phone is 303 316 2700. The homepage describes Toro Latin Kitchen and Lounge on the property and a walk to Cherry Creek North shops. The FAQ says accommodations include 193 guest rooms, 5 suites, and 1 presidential suite. Check in is 4 p.m. Check out is 12 p.m. Overnight parking is $68. Daily parking is $30. Toro parking is $15 for two hours. Electric vehicle charging is a $10 add on. Dogs are allowed for an additional fee. The amount is not printed. Cancel for no charge before 11:59 p.m. local hotel time three days before arrival. Fees apply after that deadline. The page does not print the later penalty amount. The FAQ says Denver International Airport is 23 miles southwest of the hotel, that the hotel does not provide a shuttle, and that a taxi is estimated at 100 US dollars. Southwest does not match the airport, which sits northeast of Cherry Creek. The driving directions on the same page send you from the airport to Interstate 70 west, then south on Colorado Boulevard to 1st Avenue and Clayton Lane. Use those directions. Do not use the compass word. Toro dress is described as casual. This is not Halcyon, and it is not a walk from Union Station.

Golden Triangle

The homepage describes a Leo Villareal light installation of 22,000 pieces and a 1983 wall painting by Sol LeWitt, with work in the Portico Gallery, FIRE restaurant, and on guest room floors. It says the Denver Art Museum and the Clyfford Still Museum anchor a walkable neighborhood. The rooms page says a stay in one of 165 hotel rooms and suites. The FAQ says check in is guaranteed by 4 p.m. and checkout is at 11 a.m. Some rates are not refunded if you cancel, change, or miss the stay. Other rates allow a cancellation without charge until two days before arrival, and then charge the total price. Valet is $25 for up to 4 hours, $35 for 4 to 8 hours, and $59 overnight with in and out, effective 1 August 2025. FIRE validation is $10 for up to 3 hours with a stamp, also dated 1 August 2025. The location page still tells you to keep in mind a nightly valet of $54. We are not choosing $59 over $54. Read the page you are about to pay. Dogs are welcome at a $75 fee per stay, limit two, on a leash, and not in the bars or the restaurant except service dogs. Register the dog at the desk or the FAQ says a $250 fee can apply. Smoking in a room draws a $250 fee. A rollaway is $35 a night if one is available. The fitness center, Art in Motion, is described as 2,000 square feet and open 24 hours. The Denver Art Museum is a five minute walk on the FAQ. Broadway traffic is closer than ballpark noise. Ask for a room off Broadway if sleep matters.

RiNo

River North, north of downtown, is murals, restaurants, and former warehouse blocks. It is the right luxury core when you want a smaller design hotel and one planned late night. It is a weak place to assume a Theatre District spa is in the lobby. The Ramble Hotel, at 1280 25th Street, Denver, CO 80205, is the pick. Reservations are 720 996 6300. The homepage says it is a 50 room boutique hotel in the River North Art District, with Death and Co on the property, and it names an event space called Vauxhall. The rooms page says walls were built double wide, with high performance windows, to limit noise from inside and outside the hotel. That is the hotel claim. We do not turn it into a guarantee. The FAQ says you must be over 18 to check in, with a photo identification that matches the reservation and a Visa, Mastercard, American Express, or Discover card. Cash is not accepted for the room. The card is authorized for the remaining room and tax plus $100 a night, or only the $100 if the stay is prepaid. There is no resort fee. The hotel is not pet friendly. Service dogs are accepted. The FAQ says emotional support animals are not treated as service animals under its reading of the ADA. That is the hotel wording. We are not giving a legal opinion. Smoking draws a $500 room recovery fee. Valet is $45 a night and $20 for day parking, in an on site underground garage, with unlimited in and out. Wi Fi is complimentary and the FAQ prints 300 Mbps. A small cardio room is open 24 hours. Cancel 72 hours before arrival or the charge is one night of room and tax. The FAQ does not print a check in clock, so we do not invent one. It lists the airport at 25.8 miles and 32 minutes via Pena Boulevard and Interstate 70, and Union Station at 1.3 miles and 6 minutes. Those minutes are the hotel planning lines. Room service runs from 7:30 a.m. through 10 p.m., and through 11 p.m. Thursday, Friday, and Saturday. A Marriott page also lists the hotel as a Design Hotels member. Timing, seasonality, and event weekends

The inventory shocks with a map attached are a sold out Saturday at Coors Field, a convention at the Colorado Convention Center, a winter holiday, and a ski weekend when downtown is the sleep and the mountains are the day. Noise, games, and the Great Hall

Union Station is lively because the hall, the trains, and the restaurants are the product. The Crawford sits in that building. A higher loft or a room off the busiest part of the hall is a different request from a room that opens onto the evening. The Oxford is the calmer historic interior, and 17th Street is still LoDo. The Maven is the game hotel on this list because the FAQ puts Coors Field a block away and puts Kachina Cantina at the door. RiNo is lively because the district is the product. The Ramble wants you there for Death and Co, and it also says the rooms were built to cut noise. Ask which rooms face the courtyard. Cherry Creek is the calmer sleep on an ordinary night. Halcyon moves the social hour to the roof until 9 p.m. Hotel Clio moves it to Toro. The Golden Triangle is quieter than a Rockies evening, and FIRE plus Broadway are still real. Four Seasons is quieter when no show is letting out across the street, and it is not quiet when the complex is full. The Brown Palace is quieter than Wynkoop and it is not a silent museum. Afternoon tea, the atrium, and a January steer in that atrium are the reason people book it. We do not invent decibel scores.

If you want the ballpark for one planned evening, sleep at the Maven or the Crawford and ask for a quieter room in the booking record. If you want the museums as the day and not as a nightlife base, sleep at the ART. If you want shopping and a pool, sleep at Halcyon and do not expect to walk home from Larimer Square. Tell the hotel which side you want. A phone request that never lands on the reservation is not a plan. Halcyon gives you 72 hours. The Oxford gives you about 48 hours. Where we could not read a window, ask while the rate is still flexible.

How to shape the weekend

A clean luxury weekend has one sleep neighborhood and one intentional outing off that map. Land, check the official room location, and do not schedule Union Station, Cherry Creek, the Golden Triangle, RiNo, and a mountain pass on the same arrival evening. If you sleep at the Crawford, give the station and one LoDo dinner the first night, and treat Cherry Creek or the museums as a chosen ride. If you sleep at the Oxford, keep 17th Street as the historic base and walk to the station on purpose. If you sleep at the Maven, let Dairy Block and one ballpark or alley night be the plan. If you sleep at Four Seasons, give the arts complex or the spa the first evening and ask which rooms face the street. If you sleep at the Brown Palace, give the atrium one unhurried sit, and book tea before you arrive if that is the point. If you sleep at Halcyon or Hotel Clio, keep Cherry Creek as the neighborhood you already chose. If you sleep at the ART, give the museums the day and LoDo one dinner.
