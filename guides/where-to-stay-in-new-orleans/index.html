Where to stay in New Orleans: best places to stay by neighborhood

By Noravelle Stays Editorial. Updated October 2026.

The best place to stay in New Orleans is not automatically the French Quarter, and it is not a room on Bourbon Street. A first visit still sleeps in the Quarter, on Royal, Dauphine, or Chartres. Couples who want quiet luxury sleep in the Garden District. Nightlife keeps Bourbon as a planned outing. The Warehouse District is the modern hotel between those two maps.

French Quarter courtyards

This is where to stay in New Orleans for a first visit: Jackson Square, Royal Street, and one intentional night out, without sleeping on Bourbon. Royal, Dauphine, and Chartres can still be loud on a festival Saturday. Ask for a courtyard room.

Hotel Monteleone

The official site lists 214 Royal Street. Royal is one block off Bourbon. That is the reason to choose it and the reason to ask for a room that does not sit over the bar. It is a famous Quarter hotel, not a Garden District porch.

Audubon Cottages

The official site lists 509 Dauphine Street and presents the property as a French Quarter hotel. Dauphine is calmer than Bourbon and still a Quarter street. A small cottage set sells through on an anniversary week while a Canal Street hotel still shows rooms. Ask which cottage faces the court.

Hotel Provincial

The official site includes 1024 Chartres Street. Chartres in the lower Quarter is a different night from a Royal Street door. The hotel describes courtyards. Confirm parking and the cancellation window on the site. It is not a room on Bourbon.

Canal Street and the river

Canal Street is the seam between the Quarter and the Central Business District. It is busy. It is not Bourbon. Use it when you want a larger hotel and a walk into the Quarter that does not start on the loudest block.

The Ritz-Carlton, New Orleans

The official overview includes 921 Canal Street. It is the full service hotel on that seam. You gain more than one room type and a walk into the Quarter. You give up a cottage. Ask for a quieter room on a wedding or meeting day.

NOPSI Hotel

The hotel lists 317 Baronne Street. It says the stay is in the Central Business District, less than a five minute walk from Bourbon and the French Quarter, in the former New Orleans Public Service building, with the rooftop bar and pool Above the Grid. The roof is a feature and a noise source. Ask for a room off it if the walk is the reason you came and the lounge is not.

Four Seasons Hotel New Orleans

The hotel lists 2 Canal Street. Its own page describes a riverfront tower on Canal and also places the hotel in the Central Business District, a short ride from the convention center, with a rooftop pool. Read that as a river and Canal address, not as a Dauphine cottage. Ask which room faces the river and which faces the roof.

Warehouse and Arts District

This is the best area to stay in New Orleans when you want the Quarter nearby and you do not want Bourbon as the lobby soundtrack. Museums and Magazine Street restaurants are the day. The streetcar and a rideshare cover the Garden District.

The Higgins Hotel

The official site lists 1000 Magazine Street and calls the hotel the official hotel of the National WWII Museum, in the Arts and Warehouse District. The museum is the reason to sleep here. The hotel also describes meeting space. Ask whether an event is in the building on your dates.

The Eliza Jane

The official site includes 315 Magazine Street. Some of the hotel’s marketing uses French Quarter language. The address we could read is Magazine Street, which is the Warehouse District side of Canal, a short walk toward the Quarter, not a courtyard on Royal. Ask for a room off the lobby bar if you came for the museums.

Maison Métier

The official site includes 546 Carondelet Street. It is the small Warehouse District guesthouse in this set. A house scale room can sell out on a festival week while a larger Magazine Street hotel still shows inventory. Ask whether the room faces the quieter side of the street. We do not copy award claims from the hotel onto this page.

Garden District and the Lower Garden District

This is the quiet luxury answer. The St. Charles streetcar is the daytime link to Canal. It is not a private car, and it is not a promise during a parade. Check the RTA before you build a dinner on one departure. The Quarter becomes a planned outing. Walk that shape with the Garden District day plan.

Pontchartrain Hotel

The official site presents the hotel as a Garden District stay, and the page text includes St. Charles Avenue. Calmer nights than Bourbon are the point. A street facing room on that avenue is also a parade balcony when floats roll. Ask for a quieter side if you are in town for Mardi Gras and you still want to sleep.

Henry Howard Hotel

The official site includes 2041 Prytania Street. It is the Lower Garden District house in this set, with Magazine Street nearby. A small mansion sells through on a wedding weekend while a larger hotel still shows rooms. Prytania is residential. It is not silent in parade season. Tell the hotel if a street facing room is the wrong request, and read the total on the site even when a fee line says an extra is not added.

Hotel Saint Vincent

The official site titles the hotel as a Lower Garden District hotel, and the page includes 1507 Magazine Street. Magazine shopping is at the door. It is livelier than a purely residential block and calmer than Bourbon. Ask which room faces the street and which faces the court if sleep matters more than the terrace.

FAQ

Where should I stay in New Orleans for the first time?

Stay in the French Quarter, on Royal, Dauphine, or Chartres, not on Bourbon Street. Hotel Monteleone is at 214 Royal Street. Audubon Cottages are at 509 Dauphine Street. Hotel Provincial’s site includes 1024 Chartres Street. Ask for a courtyard room or a room off the bar. The Quarter is the walk. Silence is a request.

What is the best area to stay in New Orleans for couples?

Choose Audubon Cottages at 509 Dauphine Street when you want a small Quarter stay. Choose the Garden District when the night should be calmer than Bourbon. Pontchartrain Hotel’s site presents it as a Garden District hotel on St. Charles Avenue. Henry Howard Hotel’s site includes 2041 Prytania Street. Ask which room faces the quieter side.

Where should I stay in New Orleans for nightlife?

Keep Bourbon as an outing, not the pillow. Hotel Monteleone at 214 Royal Street is one block off Bourbon. NOPSI Hotel, at 317 Baronne Street, says it is in the Central Business District and less than a five minute walk from Bourbon, with the rooftop Above the Grid. Ask for a room off the bar or the roof.

Where is the quiet luxury stay in New Orleans?

The Garden District and the Lower Garden District are the calmer nights. Pontchartrain Hotel is the St. Charles Avenue hotel in this set. Henry Howard Hotel is at 2041 Prytania Street. Hotel Saint Vincent, at 1507 Magazine Street, calls itself a Lower Garden District hotel. Magazine is livelier than a porch and still calmer than Bourbon. Parade nights on St. Charles are the exception. Ask for a quieter side.
