What are hotel resort fees?

A hotel resort fee (sometimes called a destination fee or amenity fee) is a mandatory per-night charge that many properties add on top of the advertised room rate. It is not a tip and it is usually not optional. The true nightly cost is the room rate plus the fee plus taxes.

Why the fee exists

US hotels have used mandatory resort and destination fees for years, especially in leisure markets and large convention cities. The American Hotel and Lodging Association describes a large, competitive lodging industry where properties package amenities in different ways; see AHLA Our Industry. Separating a base room rate from a mandatory amenity fee can make the headline rate look lower while shifting part of the cost into a second line item.

US consumer agencies have long warned travelers to look past the first number. The Federal Trade Commission’s hotel fee guidance tells consumers to watch for mandatory fees that appear after the room rate and to compare the total price, not only the teaser rate. See the FTC’s consumer materials on hotel resort fees and drip pricing practices via the FTC October 2023 junk fees rule proposal and the Unfair or Deceptive Fees rulemaking materials. State attorneys general have also pressed chains on clearer disclosure. The legal landscape shifts; the practical habit for travelers does not: read the fee line before you book.

What the fee usually includes

Hotels commonly say the fee covers some mix of Wi-Fi, fitness center access, local or toll-free calls, in-room water, newspaper, pool access, or a shuttle. That list is marketing, not a standard. Two hotels in the same city can charge different amounts for different bundles. Always open the “fees” or “resort fee includes” text on the rate page.

  • Mandatory: charged for the stay whether or not you use the amenity.
  • Per room, per night: the usual structure; confirm whether it is per person.
  • Taxable: many jurisdictions tax the fee like room revenue; the checkout total rises again.
  • Different from parking or resort activities: valet parking, spa treatments, and paid tours are usually separate optional charges.

How to compare the real nightly cost

When you shortlist luxury or boutique stays in Miami, Nashville, Austin, or Charleston, build one simple number for each property:

  1. Advertised room rate
  2. Plus mandatory resort or destination fee
  3. Plus estimated taxes
  4. Equals your comparison rate for that night

Booking tips that actually help

  • Expand the full price breakdown on OTAs and on the hotel site. Screenshot or note the fee before you continue.
  • Ask whether a package, AAA, senior, or loyalty rate folds the fee into a clearer total.
  • If you will not use the listed amenities, still treat the fee as real money unless the hotel confirms a waiver in writing.
  • At checkout, check the folio for the fee name. Catch mistakes before you leave the desk.

Resort fee FAQ

Are hotel resort fees optional?

Usually no. A resort or destination fee labeled mandatory is part of the stay cost. Optional spa or parking charges are separate.

What do resort fees usually cover?

Common lists include Wi-Fi, gym access, local calls, water, or pool access. Inclusions differ by hotel, so read the property’s own description.

How do I compare hotels when one adds a resort fee?

Add the mandatory fee and taxes to each room rate, then compare those all-in nightly totals.

Can I get a resort fee waived?

Sometimes, through loyalty status or a request at booking. Never assume a waiver when you are still comparing prices.
